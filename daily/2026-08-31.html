Cybersecurity · Actionable

PaperCut's emergency patch is necessary, but it is not yet an ordinary release

Public-facing NG and MF servers should be restricted now, updated to Emergency Patch Release 2 and reviewed for activity that predates the fix.

Vendor status updated August 31 · 4-minute read

PaperCut is still treating fixes for CVE-2026-82078 and CVE-2026-81578 as an emergency response rather than a completed normal release. The company says unauthenticated requests can reach administrative backend actions before access checks finish, allowing configuration changes under specific conditions. NHS England says the pair may be chained toward code execution.

Release 2 is available for versions 24, 25 and 26 and adds hardening beyond the original patch. PaperCut recommends installing it even where Release 1 is already present. The company has not completed its usual release process, and it continues to tell customers to restrict public web access to trusted addresses regardless of whether suspicious activity has been seen.

The operational detail is awkward but important. Site and secondary print servers need a patched version along with the primary application server. Some customers using external Card or ID lookups and Security Assertion Markup Language have reported trouble after patching. For legacy SQL Server lookups, PaperCut recommends moving from the jTDS driver to Microsoft's supported Java Database Connectivity driver.

Indicators of compromise were expanded Sunday. A clean vulnerability scan therefore does not answer the incident question. If compromise is suspected, PaperCut recommends preserving backups, rebuilding the application server and restoring from a known-clean backup.

Administrator check

Remove public exposure first. Apply Release 2 across applicable servers, test authentication and card-lookup workflows, then review the new indicators and logs from before the patch.

Sources: PaperCut emergency bulletin NHS England cyber alert

Copyright · Artificial Intelligence · Lawsuit

The music publishers' new Anthropic case aims at the library, not only the output

Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell allege deliberate acquisition and use of tens of thousands of protected compositions. Anthropic denies the claims.

Complaint filed August 28 · 3-minute read

Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell Music have sued Anthropic, Chief Executive Officer Dario Amodei and co-founder Benjamin Mann in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California. The complaint alleges that Anthropic torrented, scraped and downloaded copyrighted works to build and operate Claude, and that the models can reproduce protected material. Those are allegations, not findings.

The scale distinguishes this filing from earlier music cases. The publishers say tens of thousands of compositions are involved, while a separate BMG action concerns 493. A song can also carry separate copyrights in its composition, lyrics and sound recording, increasing the number of rights and potential claims around the same commercial work.

The central legal split is familiar but increasingly concrete. Training can be defended as a transformative analytical use, while obtaining source material from an unauthorized library creates a separate copying question. The complaint appears designed to keep those issues distinct and to frame the method of acquisition as intentional conduct.

Anthropic says it disagrees and will defend itself. The next useful evidence will be the filed answer, motions defining which claims survive and any discovery that tests the complaint's account of acquisition and model behavior.

Sources: Complaint report and filing link Axios report and Anthropic response

Microsoft OneDrive · Administrator Note

OneDrive's new exclusions shift a default decision toward users

Commercial users will be able to exclude their own file types unless administrators apply a new policy to preserve the current managed experience.

Rollout completing in early September · 3-minute read

Microsoft is rolling out user-managed file-level exclusions in OneDrive for Windows and macOS. Users cannot remove exclusions set by the organization, but they can add or remove their own by default. The new DisableChangesToODIgnoreList policy blocks that self-service behavior and hides its interface.

A second policy, DisableDefaultODIgnoreList, turns off Microsoft's built-in list where an organization needs normally excluded formats to sync. Beginning Oct. 5, newly created .db-wal temporary database files will join that default list; files already syncing are unaffected.

The choice is small but real. User-managed exclusions can reduce churn and avoid uploading temporary application data. They can also create incomplete backups or records if staff exclude files without understanding a workflow. Administrators should decide deliberately whether self-service fits their support and retention model before rollout completes.

Administrator check

Review the two new policies, decide who should control exclusions and document any application that depends on .db-wal files before Oct. 5.

U.S. + World

The Lead · International Security · Developing

Renewed strikes turn the Hormuz problem military again

The exchange is limited so far, but it punctures a monthlong lull just as Washington tries to convert battlefield pressure into an allied sanctions coalition.

Current August 31 · 4-minute read

U.S. forces struck two Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday, the first known U.S. attack on Iran since late July. Iran said it retaliated against U.S. bases in Jordan, and the United Arab Emirates reported intercepting an Iranian drone over its waters Monday. The claims and damage assessments remain uneven, but the exchange marks a material change from the recent military lull.

Oil responded immediately. Brent crude rose above $90 a barrel Monday, and U.S. equities opened lower. The strait carried roughly one-fifth of world petroleum shipments before the conflict; repeated low-level disruption can keep a risk premium in fuel and shipping even without a sustained closure.

The economic consequences are already domestic. AAA says the national gasoline average remained above $4 for every day in August for the first time. Higher energy costs have complicated the Federal Reserve's inflation fight, and markets now expect at least one rate increase before year-end.

At the Group of 20 finance meeting in Asheville, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is asking allies to support a deeper campaign against Iran and says another bank will be sanctioned this week. The European Union has expressed support, but U.S. tariff fights with allies make coalition management harder. Military escalation and economic isolation are therefore moving together even though each can undermine the diplomacy needed for the other.

The threshold to watch is sustained expansion: additional targets, verified damage to energy infrastructure, persistent attacks on Gulf states or sanctions that materially change Iran's access to buyers and finance. One exchange raises risk; it does not by itself establish a new full-scale phase.

Sources: Associated Press market and strike report Associated Press G20 report Reuters oil report

Quantum Computing · Research

A useful quantum claim includes a way to trust the answer

IBM and University of Chicago researchers say 70 logical qubits completed a classically impractical sampling task in about 15 minutes while producing statistical evidence of fidelity.

Research published July 30 · resurfaced this weekend · 2-minute read

Claims of quantum advantage often leave an uncomfortable question: if a classical machine cannot reproduce the calculation, how do researchers know the quantum computer answered correctly? IBM and University of Chicago researchers designed their recent experiment around both halves of that problem.

The team encoded 70 logical qubits and ran 2,415 logical two-qubit operations plus 468 logical T gates. The quantum system completed the sampling task in roughly 15 minutes, while the researchers say leading classical simulation methods faced infeasible runtimes. A verification method provided statistical evidence that the logical circuit maintained high fidelity.

This is a research milestone, not a general-purpose machine suddenly outperforming ordinary computers at useful office or scientific work. Its value is methodological: a credible advantage claim needs a hard task, error correction and a validation path. The paper offers a concrete version of all three.

Source: IBM and University of Chicago research announcement
