The Lead · Agent Security

NVIDIA’s agent-safety platform is useful precisely because it treats the runtime as the control point

The company’s Open Agent Safety Platform combines OpenShell, an open-source runtime, with Sentry, a reference design for separate monitoring. Its central claim is architectural: permission enforcement should live outside the agent process.

Announced September 28 · 4-minute read

NVIDIA says OpenShell runs each agent in an isolated sandbox without direct network access, limits the files it can reach, and routes requests through a secured supervisory channel. It also describes policy verification intended to detect access expansion before a changed policy is accepted. Sentry adds out-of-band monitoring on NVIDIA’s BlueField-4 data processing unit in the reference design.

The distinction matters. An agent that can modify its own execution environment, secrets or network policy is being asked to honor its own limits. A supervisor that owns those controls creates a harder boundary, and an audit record of allows and denials provides a better starting point for review.

There are important qualifications. The offering is a reference system and its full hardware-monitoring design relies on NVIDIA infrastructure. Open-source code and a partner list do not establish that a deployment is secure. Organizations still need to define the human or service identity behind each agent, its smallest useful permissions, the tools it may call, review points for policy changes and an incident response path.

The immediate practical use is as a design test. If a proposed agent cannot be placed behind independently enforced network, credential and tool boundaries, it is not ready for a high-trust workflow simply because its prompts appear well behaved.

Federal Judiciary · Current Context

The judiciary’s AI work remains anchored in authority, accountability and system security

The Judicial Conference’s Sept. 17 update is not today’s news, but it is the most useful current primary-source statement on how federal courts are approaching AI as technology and governance work continue in parallel.

Published September 17 · 3-minute read

The Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts says its AI task force has identified more than 60 issues and formed seven subject-matter groups. Interim guidance is already in place. The key boundary is straightforward: core judicial functions, including decision-making and adjudication, cannot be delegated to AI, and users remain accountable for work performed with its assistance.

That statement has value beyond the judiciary. It does not treat reliable output as a property to be assumed from a model. It assigns responsibility where authority is exercised and connects governance to the surrounding systems: access, records, training and security.

The update also describes accelerated modernization of Case Management/Electronic Case Files and a goal to extend continuous diagnostics and mitigation coverage across judiciary IT. Taken together, the work recognizes that AI use cannot be separated cleanly from the integrity and security of the systems that hold filings and case records.

U.S. + World

Iran · Economic Pressure

New sanctions arrive as Iran’s currency falls and diplomacy stays unsettled

The United States announced sanctions on 10 people and entities it says support Iran’s defense supply chain. The action comes as the rial hits another low and Iranian officials say the next U.S. response to diplomatic contact has not yet been delivered.

Updated September 29 · 3-minute read

The new designations are a concrete escalation of the economic campaign, aimed at alleged procurement activity across Iran, Hong Kong and Pakistan. They do not by themselves establish a path out of the conflict. Public reporting instead describes diplomacy as unresolved, with no published terms, timetable or verified de-escalation mechanism.

The currency decline is a separate but related signal. It reflects the strain on Iran’s domestic economy and makes the war’s costs more immediate for households and businesses. It is not a clean measure of policy success: currencies move for many reasons, and sanctions can impose pain without producing the political outcome their sponsors seek.

The next test is whether either side identifies a channel that can reduce military risk while allowing commitments to be checked. Until then, the sanctions campaign and the diplomatic track should be treated as parallel, not as evidence that a settlement is near.

Source: Associated Press: Iran’s currency and new sanctions
